## Executive Summary

Nikotin Away already has more of the requested foundation than a from-scratch read of the request would suggest. It is **not** a simple survey-and-timer app: it already has 14 SQLite tables of behavioral telemetry, a rule-based dynamic-weight-learning risk engine, a smoking-time prediction engine, an unpredictable-moment scheduler (JITAI-style), and a mentor-message layer. The gap is not "build an adaptive coach from zero" — it's **(1) give the user visibility/control over what already runs silently, (2) fill three genuinely-missing subsystems (sleep, location, activity) using the cheapest defensible method for each, and (3) consolidate scattered settings into a real settings/permissions architecture.**

The research phase validated several of the user's instincts (JITAI/cue-based intervention, passive phone-sensor sleep estimation, mic-based respiratory assessment as an emerging technique) and pushed back on others (raw location tracking, per-user deep learning, three independent difficulty dials, wearable integration before step-counting exists). Recommendations below explain each call.

**Bottom line recommendation**: do NOT bolt on more sensors before building the **Settings & Permissions Center** (Phase 1) and **consolidating the existing behavior engine** (Phase 2). Every new sensor (sleep, location, activity) becomes a *harder* privacy and trust problem without a place to disclose and control it first. Sequence privacy-sensitive, novel-tech features (location, voice-based breath analysis, wearables) last, not first.

## Research Findings (sourced)

**1. Passive sleep estimation without wearables** is an active, validated research area. The standard method: classify sleep/wake in short windows (e.g. 10 minutes) using accelerometer + screen on/off + charging state + (optionally) ambient light/mic, comparing coverage against a minimum threshold (~60% of a 24h window) for reliability. Phone-on-nightstand accelerometer can even estimate breathing rate/apnea events (F1 0.89–0.96 in one study), but that requires the phone to be positioned appropriately, which isn't guaranteed. **No dedicated wearable or new permission is required** — this app's existing `sensor_usage_events` table (idle minutes, charging, accelerometer/gyroscope magnitude, screen unlocks) already contains the exact signals this technique needs.
[A Comprehensive Review of Home Sleep Monitoring Technologies (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11945902/) · [Monitoring sleep with a smartphone accelerometer](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/255703081_Monitoring_sleep_with_a_smartphone_accelerometer) · [Comparison of Sleep Features Across Smartphone Sensors, Actigraphy, and Diaries (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12352797/)

**2. Battery-efficient location** means Android's own **Geofencing API** (built on the Fused Location Provider), not manual polling. It uses low-power signals (cell/Wi-Fi), throttles background location to a few fixes/hour, and Google's own guidance shows up to 10x better battery behavior than continuous tracking. Recommended radius for significant places (home/work) is 100–150m — coarser is *more* battery-efficient and *more* privacy-defensible, not a compromise.
[About background location and battery life (Android Developers)](https://developer.android.com/develop/sensors-and-location/location/battery) · [Create and monitor geofences (Android Developers)](https://developer.android.com/develop/sensors-and-location/location/geofencing) · [Android Geofencing in 2026 (Smartupworld)](https://smartupworld.com/android-geofencing/)

**3. Voice/microphone-based respiratory assessment** is genuinely current research, not a fringe idea — 2025 work validated smartphone-microphone respiratory-rate estimation (nasal sound analysis, <1% median error even 30cm from the nose) and AI-driven vocal biomarkers predicting spirometry parameters from standardized read-aloud text, including a study where COPD patients recorded standardized text over 9 months for home FEV1 estimation. **This validates the user's instinct** — but it needs actual **audio waveform capture**, not the decibel-only `noise_meter` package currently in the project, and raises a materially higher privacy bar than a stopwatch (see Risks).
[Smartphone-based lung function measurement using AI-driven vocal biomarkers (ERS)](https://publications.ersnet.org/content/erj/66/suppl69/pa4967) · [Estimation of Respiratory Rates Using the Built-in Microphone (PubMed)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26415194/) · [A software-based lung function assessment tool: VoiceMed interview](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/17434440.2025.2514006)

**4. JITAI (Just-In-Time Adaptive Intervention) for smoking cessation** is an established research pattern: sensor/cue-triggered, in-the-moment support beats fixed-schedule reminders, and notification-timing/fatigue is explicitly called out as the thing to get right. **This app's fake-call barrier + unpredictable-moment scheduler is already architecturally a JITAI system** — the research says refine it (better cue detection, better timing), not replace it.
[A JITAI for Smoking Cessation: Feasibility and Acceptability (ScienceDirect)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0306460322002337) · [Quit Sense JITAI feasibility trial protocol (PMC)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8076923/)

**5. On-device ML / personalization**: 2025 practice for mobile personalization is on-device inference for privacy (no raw data leaves the device), but for **a single user's own behavioral stream** (a few hundred data points a month at most), a per-user neural network is the wrong tool — insufficient data, and it's an interpretability/debugging nightmare compared to weighted rule-based scoring. The "self-learning" requirement is better satisfied by **expanding the existing dynamic-weight-learning approach** (`BehaviorEngine.learnDynamicWeightsFromRecentHistory`) than by introducing TensorFlow Lite or similar.
[How Mobile Developers Can Leverage TinyML & On-Device AI in 2025](https://medium.com/@deepak.srivastava.india/how-mobile-developers-can-leverage-tinyml-on-device-ai-in-2025-5628985df4e2) · [On-Device AI Models: Privacy-First ML for Mobile Applications (ResearchGate)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/387706168_On-Device_AI_Models_Advancing_Privacy-First_Machine_Learning_for_Mobile_Applications)

**6. Regulatory**: step counts, sleep patterns, and location become **sensitive health-category data (GDPR Art. 9)** the moment they feed a wellness score or recommendation — which is exactly what this app would do with them. Explicit, granular, per-feature consent is required, data must stay purpose-limited (a sleep feature "shouldn't ask for location"), and the EU AI Act (2025–2026) adds algorithmic-transparency obligations for health-adjacent AI features. This is a direct, concrete argument *for* Phase 1 (Permissions Center) before any new sensor is added.
[GDPR for digital health: developing EU privacy-compliant apps (Chino.io)](https://www.chino.io/post/do-fitness-tracking-apps-need-to-be-complied-with-data-protection-law) · [Ensuring GDPR Compliance in Wellness and Mental Health Apps](https://www.gdpr-advisor.com/ensuring-gdpr-compliance-in-personalized-wellness-and-mental-health-apps/)

## Where I'm Pushing Back on the Original Spec

1. **Location precision** — request asks to "learn frequent places, detect smoking environments." Recommend **geofence-based place clustering (5–8 significant places max, Android Geofencing API, 100–150m radius)**, not continuous GPS logging. Coarser is both cheaper on battery and easier to defend under GDPR purpose-limitation. Continuous tracking is not needed and shouldn't be built.
2. **Voice-based breath analysis** — validated by research, but it's a **Phase 8+ opt-in enhancement, not a replacement** for the current stopwatch test. It needs real audio capture + DSP (or a third-party respiratory-analysis SDK evaluation), a materially bigger consent/retention conversation (raw voice audio must never be retained after feature extraction — process and discard), and shouldn't block anything else.
3. **Self-learning = on-device ML** — recommend **against** introducing a neural-network/TFLite pipeline. Per-user data volume doesn't support it, and it trades away the interpretability that lets the app explain *why* it thinks the risk is high (already a stated value in the existing `riskExplanation` list). Extend the existing dynamic-weight rule engine instead.
4. **Three independent difficulty dials** (Easy/Normal/Hard *and* strictness *and* kindness, all top-level) — recommend **one primary Mode selector** that sets all three together, with strictness/kindness exposed only as an "Advanced" override for users who go looking for it. Three prominent top-level sliders fragments the personalization signal and confuses most users.
5. **Wearable integration** — recommend **deferring indefinitely**, tracked only as a research spike after Phase 7 (basic step counting) ships and is actually used. Building for a hypothetical Wear OS/Health Connect audience before validating in-house step counting is premature scope for an app that doesn't have either yet.
6. **Requesting `ACCESS_FINE_LOCATION`/`ACCESS_COARSE_LOCATION` today with no feature behind them** — this is a standing risk right now (Play Store policy requires justifying every dangerous permission; App Store/Play Console review can reject unused-permission apps). Flagging for Phase 1: either build the (opt-in, disclosed) feature soon, or remove the unused permission declarations until Phase 6 actually ships.
7. **ARB/`gen-l10n` migration** for the 39-language system — real tech debt, but **low priority**. The current static-map approach works correctly today (verified extensively this session); migration cost/risk is high relative to benefit unless string volume becomes unmanageable. Not recommending this as a roadmap phase.

## Risks & Trade-offs

- **Permission creep without disclosure** is the single biggest risk right now (fine/coarse location requested, unused). Phase 1 must land before Phase 6, not after.
- **Battery**: every new phase after 3 touches a sensor. Sleep (Phase 4) reuses existing snapshot cadence — near-zero marginal battery cost. Location (Phase 6) must use Geofencing API, never a foreground polling loop. Steps (Phase 7) should use the OS step-counter sensor (hardware batched, near-zero cost), not raw accelerometer integration. Audio (Phase 8) is the only phase with a real battery/CPU cost (DSP) — should be user-initiated only (like today's breath test), never background/passive.
- **Data volume vs. personalization**: per-user ML is the wrong tool (see research); expect the rule-based engine's ceiling to be "good structural predictions," not "clinically precise." Set that expectation with the user now.
- **Regulatory exposure**: location + sleep + (future) health-adjacent voice data are GDPR Art. 9 sensitive category once used for scoring. Phase 1's per-feature consent isn't optional polish — it's the compliance mechanism.
- **Scope risk**: this is a 9-phase roadmap. Recommend treating Faz 5.4/5.5 (already in flight) and this roadmap as sequential, not parallel, unless the user explicitly wants to interleave.

- Phase 4 — Sleep Intelligence v1. **Scope note**: mid-phase investigation found the original Phase 0 research premise ("estimate sleep from existing `sensor_usage_events`") didn't hold — that table is only populated while the app's Dart isolate is alive (foreground/brief background), so a real overnight gap would just be silence, not idle-state data. Presented this to the user with two options (analysis-only vs. real background collection); user chose real background collection. Built: a native AlarmManager-driven probe (`SleepProbeReceiver.kt`) that wakes every ~45 min overnight, reads only screen-interactive + charging state (both free/cached OS state, no sensor listener, no new dangerous permission), and writes directly to SQLite even with the Flutter engine not running — mirroring the existing `NoResponseWatchdogService`/native-SQLite-write pattern. A boot receiver re-arms the schedule after reboot. Opt-in only, off by default, with a disclosure card (what/why) in Settings, consistent with Phase 1's Permissions Center pattern. `SleepIntelligenceEngine` (pure, testable) requires ≥60% of expected overnight probes present before trusting an estimate (per the Phase 0 research's reliability threshold) — otherwise it transparently falls back to the user's static survey sleep time. The estimate (when trusted) now feeds `BehaviorEngine.calculateProfileRiskAdjustment`'s short-sleep-window check in place of the static field.

- Phase 5 — Reporting System. Fixed a real pre-existing bug found along the way: `pdf: ^3.11.3` was declared under `dev_dependencies` even though report generation runs in production — moved to `dependencies`. Added `printing` (same authors as `pdf`, the standard pairing) for the native share/print sheet, so no custom file-provider/native code was needed. New `ReportEngine` (pure, DB-free, reuses `BehaviorEngine`'s existing breath/smoking trend calculators rather than re-deriving them) aggregates already-collected data into a rolling 7-day/30-day `PeriodReport` — cigarettes logged, risk/breath/smoking trends, task completion, survey/breath-test counts, days smoke-free. `ReportsPage` (reachable from the home screen's quick-menu, "Raporlar") shows it in-app with weekly/monthly toggle, and can preview/print or share it as a generated PDF via `PdfReportService`.
- Phase 6 — Location Intelligence v1. **Scope decision**: presented the user a fork (foreground-only place learning vs. full background geofencing with `ACCESS_BACKGROUND_LOCATION`) — user chose full background geofencing, the heavier/original option. Re-added `ACCESS_FINE_LOCATION`/`ACCESS_COARSE_LOCATION`/`ACCESS_BACKGROUND_LOCATION` (removed in Phase 1 for being unused; now genuinely used) plus `play-services-location`. Built: `PlaceClusteringEngine` (pure, testable) folds one foreground location sample at a time into a running, in-memory-only match-or-create against up to 8 significant places — **no raw location history is ever persisted**, only the small set of finalized place centroids, matching the roadmap's data-minimization principle. `GeofenceTransitionReceiver.kt` (native, Play Services `GeofencingClient`) registers/receives ENTER/EXIT transitions and writes `{placeId, transitionType, timestamp}` — never coordinates — directly to SQLite, same posture as the Phase 4 sleep prober. Opt-in, off by default, disclosed both in a dedicated Settings page and in the Permissions Center (this is the first phase to actually re-introduce a dangerous permission the roadmap had flagged as a standing risk in Phase 0). **Known limitation found during on-device testing**: the end-to-end pipeline (permission flow → foreground location sampling → place learning → native geofence registration call) was verified working — including watching a real `SignificantPlace` get created and persisted from a live GPS fix — but Play Services itself rejected the actual geofence registration on the test device (`Geofencer: registration not active, registration not permitted`, confirmed via logcat with the exact correct coordinates/radius reaching the native call). This is a platform/device-level rejection, not a code defect — the request reaches Play Services correctly — but it means live ENTER/EXIT arrival notifications could not be confirmed firing on this device/session. Left as a flagged follow-up rather than a rabbit-hole to chase to ground now.

- Phase 7 — Activity & Steps v1. Used the device's hardware step-counter sensor (`TYPE_STEP_COUNTER`) rather than Health Connect — matches the roadmap's own battery guidance ("hardware-batched, near-zero cost"), needs no separate app to be installed, and reuses `ACTIVITY_RECOGNITION` (already granted from onboarding) so **no new permission or consent screen was needed**. Built the same daily-native-probe pattern as Phase 4/6 (`StepProbeReceiver.kt`, once shortly after local midnight) so a day-boundary cumulative-count snapshot exists even on days the app isn't opened, plus a throttled foreground read on app open. `StepTrendEngine` (pure, testable) converts the raw cumulative counter into daily deltas, correctly handling the counter reset that happens on device reboot (treats a decrease as "counter restarted" rather than producing a negative delta). Deliberately **did not** add a dedicated opt-in toggle/consent page like Sleep/Location Intelligence got — proportionate to the much lower sensitivity of an anonymous step count with no coordinates — instead updated the existing Activity permission card's disclosure text to mention step tracking. Step totals/averages now appear in the weekly/monthly report (`ReportsPage`/PDF) alongside the other trend numbers.

- **Cross-cutting fix (between Phase 7 and 8, user-requested)** — Background reliability across manufacturers. The user asked a sharp question mid-session: everything up to this point had only ever been tested on the one Xiaomi/MIUI device in hand, and MIUI-specific accommodations (autostart, lock-screen popup permission) had been built — what happens on a Samsung, Pixel, Huawei, Oppo, Vivo, OnePlus phone? Answer: most of the app (permission_handler, sensors, geofencing) already used standard cross-manufacturer Android APIs and degrades gracefully. But the real gap was background-process-killing, which is a documented Android-wide fragmentation problem (not MIUI-specific) that could silently break the AlarmManager-driven probes (Sleep/Location/Steps) on other aggressive-battery-management OEMs the same way it could on MIUI — and the app had no generalized mitigation, only a MIUI-only one built earlier for the fake-call barrier's lock-screen permission. Added the universal, manufacturer-agnostic `REQUEST_IGNORE_BATTERY_OPTIMIZATIONS` request (standard AOSP API, works identically on every Android device) as the primary fix, plus generalized the existing MIUI-only native deep-link mechanism to also cover Huawei/Honor, Oppo, Vivo, and OnePlus's known (best-effort, community-documented) autostart/protected-apps screens. **Follow-up (same session)**: the user then asked explicitly whether Samsung and other "standard" Android phones were covered — they weren't, since Samsung has no single stable autostart-screen intent the way the Chinese OEM skins do. Rather than leave Samsung (and every other manufacturer with no known screen) with just the battery-exemption button, added Samsung/Meizu/Asus best-effort candidates too, and — more importantly — a universal fallback: if no OEM-specific screen resolves, the app now opens the generic per-app settings screen (`openAppSettings`, a standard Android intent present on literally every device), so **every** manufacturer, listed or not, ends up with two actionable buttons rather than sometimes just one. Surfaced as a new "Arka Planda Çalışma" card at the top of the Permissions Center. On-device verified on the Xiaomi test device (still the only hardware available this session): manufacturer correctly detected as an aggressive one, the MIUI autostart screen opened correctly through the generalized code path both before and after the Samsung/fallback addition (confirming the fallback logic didn't regress the already-working Xiaomi path), and the universal battery-optimization-exemption request correctly triggered Android's standard system dialog (MIUI then routes it through its own battery-permission confirmation screen, which is expected MIUI behavior, not a bug). The Samsung/Meizu/Asus component names and the generic-fallback path itself could not be verified on real hardware for those manufacturers in this session.

- Phase 8 — Enhanced breath assessment. Replaced the manual stopwatch (tap-to-start, tap-to-stop) with real microphone-based acoustic exhale detection, plus a fully voice-guided step wizard. **Scope note**: per Option 2 chosen by the user from the research-spike's proposals, `holdDuration`/`blowDuration`'s underlying formulas were deliberately left unchanged (still derived from elapsed attempt time) — only `blowIntensity`/`blowStability` get replaced with real acoustic-derived values, and only when all 3 attempts have a confident acoustic reading; otherwise the pre-existing timing-based proxy is used exactly as before, so this phase never distorts the already-calibrated risk-scoring formulas or historical trend comparisons. Built `BreathAcousticEngine` (pure, testable): index-based (not wall-clock-based — real-device recorder startup latency makes wall-clock unreliable) adaptive-threshold onset/offset detection with debounce/hysteresis, median-based calibration baseline resistant to the tap/handling-noise spike that the very first audio chunk after recorder start reliably carries. `BreathAudioService` wraps the `record` package for raw PCM16 streaming; each chunk is reduced to a single RMS energy float and discarded immediately (raw audio never buffered/persisted, matching the roadmap's data-minimization principle). Added `flutter_tts`-based `BreathVoiceGuideService` for spoken instructions (device TTS, no bundled audio assets), serializing overlapping speak()/stop() calls onto a queue and reporting real playback start/stop so the mic can ignore samples while the phone's own speaker is talking (the speaker leaks into the mic on a phone; unmuted, the "exhale now" cue was literally getting picked up as the calibration baseline itself in on-device testing, making the threshold impossible for the real exhale to clear).

  The page (`breath_test_page.dart`) was substantially reworked, iteratively, against live on-device feedback into a fully voice-guided flow: read/hear "sit and relax" → OK → read/hear "take a deep breath" → OK → a real 5-second hold countdown (with a "press Başlat" confirm-and-retry gate at the end — if not tapped within 5s, assumes the hold wasn't real and restarts the countdown and the measurement clock together, so an abandoned hold can't silently inflate the saved duration) → directly into the exhale measurement (mic auto-detects the exhale, or the user taps a "Tamam" button/the circle manually) — no redundant intermediate confirmation screens. Attempt 1 requires one initial tap to begin; **attempts 2 and 3 are fully hands-free**: during each 20-second rest interval, a combined voice briefing walks through the entire next attempt in one pass, and the exhale measurement starts automatically the instant both the rest countdown *and* the announcement have finished (whichever finishes last — exact TTS duration isn't knowable ahead of time, so this is a dual-condition gate, not a fixed-offset guess). A slow big-to-small shrink animation inside the timer circle gives visual feedback during the exhale itself. ## Phase 11 — Step/Heart-Rate Data Into the Risk Score + Prolonged-Sitting Detection (2026-07-22)

**Scope delivered**: step count and heart rate now feed the actual `dynamicRisk` formula in `storage_service.dart` (previously they only appeared in reports/Settings UI, per Phase 0's re-audit finding). A new "you've been still for a while" nudge reuses the same step data. **Location-based risk was deliberately not attempted this pass** — see the dedicated note below.

1. **Step-activity risk adjustment.** `StepTrendEngine.isMostRecentDaySedentary` (new, pure) compares the most recent day's step count against the average of the prior days (needs ≥3 days of history to avoid a noisy 1-day comparison); if today is below 40% of that personal baseline, `dynamicRisk` gets a small `+3` (same scale as the existing duration-barrier/sleep adjustments) — physical activity reducing cravings is well-established, so a sudden drop from the user's own normal is treated as a small risk signal, not an absolute threshold. Reads `step_counter_samples` already being collected by Phase 7's native probe — no new I/O.
2. **Heart-rate risk adjustment — deliberately cached, not live.** Re-examined Phase 9's original deferral reasoning and confirmed it still holds: `loadBehaviorDashboard()` runs on *every* app open with no staleness check (a genuinely hot path — traced its only caller in `home_page.dart` to confirm this). Wiring a live Health Connect platform-channel read directly into it would add unpredictable native I/O latency to every single app launch for a signal most users won't have enabled. Instead: new `StorageService.refreshWearableRiskSignal()` runs out-of-band (`unawaited`, throttled to at most once/hour, same cadence `LocationIntelligenceService` already uses for foreground sampling), fetches the Health Connect snapshot, runs it through `WearableSignalEngine.isElevatedHeartRate`, and caches a small `+4` adjustment (or `0`) as a plain setting. `loadBehaviorDashboard()` only ever does a cheap synchronous-feeling `loadSetting` read of that cache — no native channel call in the hot path itself. No-ops entirely (adjustment stays `0`) when the feature is off or Health Connect has no data, matching the existing defensive posture.
3. **Prolonged-sitting nudge.** New `StorageService.isRecentlySedentary` / `StepTrendEngine.isSedentaryWindow`: true when the gap between the oldest and newest step sample in the last 3 hours is at least 90 minutes but fewer than 150 steps were taken in that span — deliberately requires a real time gap, not just "two samples with a small delta," since samples are only ever written on a throttled foreground read or the daily native probe (never continuously), so a short gap between samples says nothing about activity level either way. Wired into `home_page.dart`'s existing `_loadHomeMetrics()` notification-scheduling block (`unawaited`, alongside `_notifyNewTasks` etc.), gated by waking hours (hour-granularity, derived from the user's own sleep/wake settings) and a 3-hour cooldown persisted the same way the mandatory-gate cooldown was (a lesson directly carried over from the duplicate-notification bug fixed earlier this session — this feature was built *with* a persisted guard from the start, not retrofitted after the fact). New gentle notification (`NotificationService.showSedentaryReminderNotification`) deliberately styled like the existing breath-test reminder (normal ringtone, no insistent/full-screen alarm treatment) rather than the task notifications' alarm styling, since this is a freely-ignorable wellness suggestion, not something the discipline protocol needs an answer to.
4. **Transparency**: both new risk adjustments are appended to `riskExplanation` (the existing user-facing "why is my risk X" breakdown), matching how every other adjustment in that function is already surfaced.

**Location-based risk — explicitly deferred, not attempted.** Wiring "significant places" into the risk score properly would require correlating `location_visit_events` (place enter/exit timestamps) against historical smoking/violation events to derive which specific places are actually smoking-associated for *this* user — a real feature, not a small bounded adjustment like the two above. Attempting a shortcut version (e.g. "any visit to any learned place raises risk") would be meaningless — the whole point of place-learning is that most learned places (home, work) are *not* risk-associated. Given Phase 6 already flagged that live geofence ENTER/EXIT delivery couldn't be confirmed working on the test device (Play Services rejected registration), building a risk-scoring layer on top of a still-unconfirmed data source was judged premature. Left as a distinct future phase once (a) geofencing is confirmed actually firing on a real device, and (b) there's enough accumulated visit-history + violation data to derive a real per-place correlation rather than guessing at one.

This had almost certainly been silently broken since Phase 6 originally shipped it.

Also added defensive `.timeout()` wrappers to every step in this chain and to `GeofencingService`'s two native calls, on the reasoning that a screen depending on a device's location/Play Services stack should never be able to hang indefinitely again regardless of cause — cheap insurance once the real bug was already found and fixed.
